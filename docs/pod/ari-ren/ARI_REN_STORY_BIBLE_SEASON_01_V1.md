
# ARI & REN — STORY BIBLE v1
## ONE DESK, TWO WORLDS
Created 2026-11-11. Proposed original IP for Tiny Couple Chaos. Creative draft; not finalized or published.

> Same desk. Different worlds. Still us.

## Canon and visual lock
The reference is exactly the owner's Option 3 The Doodle Roommates image.
ARI: small, white, round simplified blob creature; yellow ribbed beanie with top nub, black dot eyes, tiny mouth, faint blush, subtle tiny hands. Slightly steadier silhouette. No human hair, anime anatomy or outfit changes.
REN: small white soft blob creature; headphones with a visible peach/orange heart on the ear cup, tiny black dot eyes and restrained mouth, bouncy posture. No hair, fashion clothing, manga eyes or replacement headphones.
Together: keep large negative space, dark hand-wobbled stroke, warm ivory, butter yellow, soft blue and peach. Props: laptop with small heart, two mugs, book stack, simple blanket and sticky notes. No mandatory background in print-ready merch artwork.

## Series premise / treatment
Ari and Ren, a couple, move into a very small apartment with only one desk. Ari needs order and marks the desk with a straight line and a neat yellow TO DO note. Ren scribbles a pale-blue door across the dividing line and adds a note: DO TOGETHER?
Each short episode is a tiny relationship problem, told mostly visually: shared chargers, stolen snacks, deadlines, weekend plans, sleep, awkward apologies. Their notes gradually cover the wall. Yellow is Ari's planner world, pale blue is Ren's spontaneous imagination, and peach appears when they build a shared ritual. Not literal magic. When the season ends, the tape splitting the desk is gone: the two different styles remain, but they belong together.

## Emotional engine
ARI: organized, reliable, often anxious about unfinished tasks; shows love by preparing things for both. Ari's weakness is over-management, not being clever. Season change: an unfinished to-do list does not make an unsuccessful day.
REN: playful, observant, clever, sometimes hides discomfort with a joke; shows love through noticing and spending time. Ren's weakness is avoiding difficult conversations and losing track of time, not being stupid. Season change: showing up consistently matters more than constant novelty.
Neither character is permanently right, is gender-coded by color, or exists solely as a punchline. Conflict never turns cruel or manipulative. The romance shows through quiet small gestures: an extra cup, one ear cup removed to listen, a hand placed beside the other, a tiny peach note.

## Distinctive recurring visual language
Ari's straight yellow arrows and boxes vs. Ren's blue crooked loops and small doodle doors.
Every resolution earns one peach note on the wall. Repetition creates a recognizable visual franchise, not dependence on captions.
The apartment is deliberately simple: shared desk, kitchenette, window, blanket corner, front door. Keep the characters small and instantly recognizable at sticker scale.

## Pilot-ready English storyboards
### Episode 1 — One Desk, Two Worlds (5-slide carousel)
1. Wide: both tiny white figures squeezed behind one desk. On-screen: ONE DESK.
2. Ari's yellow-hatted profile straightens a stack of books, makes a perfectly straight dividing line. On-screen: MY SIDE / YOUR SIDE.
3. Ren looks at the line; one headphone shows a heart. Ren draws a little blue door through the middle. Silent.
4. Ari examines the little door, shifts their mug across, then places a second mug beside it. On-screen: ...OKAY.
5. Two faces at the laptop; one peach sticky note on the wall: US. On-screen: ROOM FOR US.
Caption: We moved in together. Our desk did not get the memo. Are you the planner or the doodler?
CTA: Share this with the person who takes over your desk.
Reel adaptation: 0–2 seconds divided desk; 2–5 door scribble; 5–9 both mugs slide together, final US note.

### Episode 2 — The Last Cookie Case (4-slide carousel)
1. Ari discovers one empty plate. On-screen: THE LAST COOKIE...
2. Ari makes a hilariously serious tiny crumb evidence diagram with straight yellow arrows. On-screen: I HAVE QUESTIONS.
3. Ren pauses the music, opens a folded napkin: half the cookie is carefully wrapped. No text.
4. They share the half-cookie. On-screen: SAVED YOUR HALF.
Caption: A true crime investigation. A very small plot twist. Would you save the last bite?
Merch crop: one split cookie between their two little heads, NOT the full four panels.

### Episode 6 — The Unsent Message (5-slide emotional anchor)
1. Evening blue through window, Ari surrounded by lists and a laptop. On-screen: I'M OKAY.
2. Ari types I'M TIRED and deletes it; no large dramatic face.
3. Ren sees the silence and gently takes one headphone cup off; a tiny blue question mark.
4. Ren arrives with two cups and sits beside Ari. On-screen: TEA?
5. Ari leans against Ren. One peach sticky note on desk: NOT ALONE.
Caption: Sometimes love doesn't have a solution. Sometimes it simply makes room.
Reel adaptation: no commercial song copying, 8–12 seconds, subtle visual beats; do not promise mental health treatment.

## Signature recurring gags and audience hooks
- Ari always prepares two cups, even while claiming to want silence.
- Ren says FIVE MINUTES; the clock begs to differ.
- The last cookie becomes a detective case each month.
- Ari draws boxes; Ren draws loopholes, then occasionally surprises Ari with a beautifully tidy plan.
- Once per week a nearly silent romantic beat replaces the punchline.
- The headphone touch is Ren's visual signal: I'm listening.
- A blank peach note means a day without having to earn the right to rest.
- Audience chooses fictional mini-adventures (rainy walk or cozy cooking), never which partner is right or a better person.
